# Identity specification

Use this schema for proposed directions and the accepted `brand-identity.md`. Keep it semi-structured and readable: concise prose, labeled copy, tables, and links to actual assets. Adapt section names to the project while retaining the substance. Proposed documents may mark unresolved fields as working; the accepted document must identify any intentionally provisional decision. Include only applicable application rules.

## Status and identity at a glance

Record project name, direction/territory, proposed or accepted status, decision date, and what is fixed versus working. For an accepted identity, link the final one-pager and canonical hero near the top.

Give a compact summary containing:

- **Name:** exact spelling, casing, pronunciation when relevant, and known domain or URL status. Distinguish a suggestion, reserved domain, and verified live site.
- **Brand idea:** the central belief or organizing concept.
- **Mantra:** a compact internal or external rallying idea; specify its use.
- **Main one-liner:** the primary benefit or emotional hook.
- **Descriptive one-liner:** a literal standalone explanation of what the product is, who it helps, and what it does.
- **Main value proposition and promise:** what the audience gets and why this product can deliver it.
- **Character and primary emotion:** a few meaningful traits and the feeling to evoke.

These copy roles are distinct. An evocative mantra need not explain the category; a descriptive one-liner must. Assign a clear role to application-specific or campaign lines instead of presenting all copy as interchangeable taglines.

## Project, audience, and motivations

Summarize the project's origin and why, form factor, current functionality/scope, stage, ambitions, and success criteria. Identify the primary audience and use context; distinguish secondary audiences where they change the message. Separate shipped capabilities, planned capabilities, and brand aspiration.

Capture the problem or tension, differentiator, primary benefit, proof or reasons to believe it, and relevant alternatives. Connect the identity to the goals and desired emotional effect. Attribute supplied inspirations and explain which qualities matter. If a reference is uninspected, say so rather than inventing its relevance.

Preserve constraints with their **fixed / working / open** status and unresolved questions. Favor a short factual brief over a speculative market analysis.

## Positioning, ethos, and creative direction

Explain the chosen territory, central belief, values or motivating principles, attitude, and marketing angle. Translate vibes into decisions: what should audiences feel, and which visual and verbal behaviors create that feeling?

For a candidate, give its differentiating idea, strongest fit to the brief, and main tradeoff or risk. For the accepted system, record useful decision rationale and link to exploration history rather than carrying rejected directions as active choices.

## Name, logo, wordmark, and icon

Link native SVG masters and approved variants. Specify mark meaning and geometry, name casing, lockup, clear space, minimum usable size, light/dark or monochrome behavior, background treatment, and small-size simplification as appropriate to this identity. Note provisional lettering or required fonts honestly.

Explain which asset belongs on each surface. A logo embedded in a poster is not a reusable logo deliverable; the linked masters must exist independently.

## Color and typography

Use a color table with **token/name, exact value, role, and approved pairings**. Explain dominance and accent behavior, any light/dark adaptation, and tested text/background pairings. Separate brand colors from product states or content-driven colors when that distinction matters.

Specify type families, available weights, fallbacks, source or license notes when relevant, and roles for headlines, body, labels, and wordmark. Describe scale, spacing, and hierarchy through usable rules and samples. Record font dependencies required to reproduce the exported artifacts.

## Imagery and visual behavior

Link the canonical hero and supporting imagery. Describe subject matter, visual metaphor, medium/materials, composition, crop, lighting, texture, shape language, and density where these define the identity. Include alt text, generation provider/prompt or provenance needed to reproduce or locate assets, and known usage limitations.

Explain how the identity appears in a real touchpoint suited to the form factor. Distinguish conceptual mockups from screenshots or verified product evidence. Explain how the visual system stays recognizable as content changes.

## Voice and copy bank

Describe voice through concrete behavior: sentence shape, level of formality, vocabulary, humor, confidence, and audience relationship. Include a few paired **do / don't** copy examples explaining the difference.

Record the approved copy hierarchy, a short introduction, useful campaign/support lines, and representative CTA or interface labels. State claims boundaries based on current functionality; identify aspirations as such. Describe where the voice should become more literal, such as errors or controls.

## Application, assets, and maintenance

Give practical guidance for relevant touchpoints: product UX, marketing, GitHub README, social previews, announcements, and other customer-visible surfaces. Identify stable brand rules and the flexibility allowed within them.

Maintain an asset inventory with **purpose, relative link, format/dimensions, and variant/status**. Link the poster SVG and PNG, logo files, both favicon formats, social image, hero, and other accepted assets. Include the project-local build source, rendering inputs, exact working regeneration command, and dependency/font requirements. State how rendered fields stay synchronized with this document.

End with a short change rule: normal copy and applications may evolve within the system; changes to the accepted name, positioning, core visual language, mark, or voice require a new brand decision. Link exploration history as historical context.
